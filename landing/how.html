How it works

One session. Five minutes. A single pass.

There is no “study for three months first” step. You speak on day one, and then you do that again tomorrow.

What one session actually looks like

The Today's path card on the home screen has exactly one button. Press it and the seven steps below run in one go — no bouncing between screens looking for the next thing.

  1. Your must-say line for today — the card names the line and why it was picked (your weak spot, a due review, or the city you're travelling to).
  2. Hear it real — native speed first. If it's too fast, drop to one of four slower speeds, 0.5×–1.3×.
  3. Cover the text and say it three times — reading it off the screen doesn't count as speaking. This is the core move of the whole product.
  4. Tap the mic and get scored — recognised, scored, and told what specifically went wrong: which tone drifted, which sound was off.
  5. Two bonus lines — more lines from the same scene, same rhythm.
  6. One due review — pulled from the review ladder, timed for right before you'd forget it.
  7. A one-line wrap-up — how many lines you attempted, your average, how many you nailed. Once a node's lines are spoken well, the path advances by itself.

The whole method, in one sentence

Cover the Chinese, say it out loud three times from memory, and find out immediately whether it landed.

👀

Recognising is not producing

Understanding a line and producing it are two different abilities. Most learning products train the first one, because it's easier to build and easier to score. We only train the second.

🔁

Why three times

The first pass is recall. The second is assembling the syllables. By the third it's starting to become muscle memory. Say it once and you're just echoing.

🎯

Why the score has to be immediate

Learning tomorrow that you got it wrong is the same as not learning it — by then you've already rehearsed the wrong version. The feedback has to arrive while your mouth is still open.

Three things we deliberately don't do

Half of what a product is comes from what it adds. The other half comes from what it refuses to add.

🚫

No grammar drills

Not because grammar doesn't matter, but because ordering lunch doesn't require the structural analysis of 把-sentences first. Grammar surfaces on its own when you say the same kind of sentence enough times.

🚫

No guilt mechanics

There is a streak, because it's genuinely useful. There is no “you hurt the panda”, no emotional pressure designed to drag you back. Break the streak, pick it up tomorrow.

🚫

No lines nobody says

“The cat is on the table” is perfectly grammatical and you will never say it. Every piece of content has to pass one gate: which extra line of Chinese does this get the user to say? If it can't pass, it doesn't ship.

Your data

This section says the same thing as the privacy policy, just less formally. There are no accounts, so there is no account to delete either.

📱

Your progress lives on your device

By default everything sits in your own browser. Clear your browser data and it's gone — which is exactly why the next item exists.

☁️

One anonymous backup, for one reason

So that clearing your cache or switching devices doesn't wipe your progress. It's tagged only by an anonymous device ID stored in your browser — not your name, email or location. It contains no audio and no text you typed anywhere except the practice lines themselves.

🎙️

Audio is uploaded only when you tap the mic

It's sent to be transcribed and scored, then discarded — the raw audio isn't stored. You can skip the mic entirely and just listen and shadow.

🔔

Notifications store only what a reminder needs

If you never turn notifications on, none of this exists. If you do, what's stored is: the push address, the same anonymous device ID, your interface language and time zone, the purpose you picked, your streak and how many lines are due — plus, only if you add a trip, the city, arrival date and length of stay.

What's stored For how long How to remove it
Progress backup While you keep using it Clear browser data for sinoky.pages.dev
Notification preference record Expires after 180 days without activity Turn notifications off

No ads, no ad tracking, and nothing you do here is sold or shared. The full text is in the privacy policy; questions about it go to privacy@sinoky.app.

How it's built

This matters because it's what determines the experience you get.

⚡

One web page, served from the edge

The whole product is a web app served from edge locations worldwide — open it and go, no install, no signup. After the first load it's cached on your device, so it still works without a connection.

🤖

The Android app is the same code

The Android app is a thin native shell wrapped around exactly the same web code. The upside: when a fix lands on the web, app users get it the next time they open Sinoky — usually with no reinstall.

🧩

No third-party trackers

There are no ad scripts and no ad pixels on the page. The only things that ever leave your device are the ones listed in the section above.

Who it's for — and who it isn't

✅ It's for you if

  • You're going to China, or already living or working there
  • You're starting from zero and need to get things done, not pass an exam
  • You can spare 5–10 minutes a day, at most
  • You're willing to speak out loud, even quietly on a commute

❌ It's not for you if

  • Your goal is an HSK exam or a systematic grammar syllabus — the flashcards help a little, but this isn't built for it
  • You mainly want to train reading and handwriting
  • You want open-ended AI conversation practice — the dialogues here are six designed scenes, not unlimited free chat
  • You don't want to speak at all
