Features

Everything here exists to get you to say one more line of Chinese

No “AI-powered” and no “immersive learning” in this list. Only things you can actually tap inside the product.

Say it, and know instantly whether it landed

Tap the mic, say the line. Your speech is transcribed and scored, and the feedback names the actual problem — not a vague “pronunciation needs work”, but which tone drifted and which initial was off. Below the score you get a syllable-by-syllable breakdown and a one-tap way to try again.

  • Tone-level and sound-level error causes, not a vague score
  • Syllable-by-syllable breakdown — tap any part to hear it
  • You can't get stuck: miss the same line twice and the hint appears by itself
  • Scoring never uses altered-speed audio; slow playback only ever affects the model audio you listen to

Textbooks teach 你好. Nobody says 你好 all day.

137 must-say lines, each with two more ways people really say it — casual or polite. That's 409 recorded lines across 417 audio clips. The version you hear on the street is the one you need to understand.

Cover the text, say it three times

Blind-speak is the default move on the main path: cover the Chinese, say the line three times from memory. Reading it off the screen is easy and proves nothing; remembering it with your eyes off the text does.

Four slow speeds — only for listening

Four playback speeds from 0.5× to 1.3× help you separate tone changes and fast speech. The scoring path always uses unaltered audio — otherwise the score would be meaningless.

Ears first — you can't say a tone you can't hear

Each of the four tones own a colour, used consistently from the flashcards to the score screen, so tone stops being an abstract concept and becomes something you recognise at a glance. The listening drill makes you pick one of four, and a wrong answer comes with a plain-language explanation.

  • Four tones, four colours — one visual language across the product
  • A four-way listening drill for exactly the 妈麻马骂 problem
  • Tone change is taught too: consecutive third tones, and how 一 and 不 shift

Not a fixed course — a path that follows you

The app keeps a learning record across eight dimensions — tone, initials, finals, fluency, listening, vocabulary, grammar, pragmatics — and weights tomorrow's practice toward whichever ones you struggled with. It also tells you why each line was chosen: it hits your weak spot, it's due for review, it matches the city you're travelling to, or it's simply new. Every term is spelled out on the card. No black box.

  • Six adaptive placement questions decide where you start
  • A “Today's path” card turns your goal into a route you tick off
  • One continuous session you can finish without bouncing between screens
  • Progress follows you to a second device

Scenario Coach: from repeating a line to holding a conversation

Six high-frequency scenes, played turn by turn. You play your own side; the app plays the waiter, the vendor, the driver, the receptionist, the neighbour — until the job is actually done.

The scene you least want to need — and most need to have practised.

Every line you have to say comes from the library you're already learning — nothing new is invented just to keep a dialogue going. The work is putting those lines to use.

Lines come back right before you'd forget them

Everything you speak enters a review ladder — 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days — scheduled without you managing it. Today's plan quietly folds in whatever has come due.

  • The ladder schedules itself; you never build a review list
  • Miss the same line three times and it changes approach — sound by sound, or a different way to say it
  • After a trip you get a retro card: how many lines you said, your average, what tripped you up, and the first line to say next time

18 cities of scenes, 3 cities of full guides

Beijing, Shanghai and Chengdu each have a full guide: nine real-world zones — landing, mobile payment, metro transfers, taxis, ordering, tickets, check-in, asking for help, departure and tax refunds. Each zone has key phrases, a situation dialogue and “say it 3×” tracking.

Every zone has a dialogue

Not a word list — a dialogue you'd actually take part in.

Ask Nono, the local guide

Each zone has a one-tap question button, and the answer comes with your current city as context.

Cities are parameterised

Adding a city is one data entry with no front-end change — which is how 18 city sets ship alongside three deep guides.
